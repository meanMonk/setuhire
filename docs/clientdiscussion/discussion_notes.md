# Discussion Notes — SetuHire Proposal (Internal Use Only)

Not for client. Talking points, ROI framing, and questions to ask on the call.

---

## Approach: ask before you pitch

Don't lead with the value pitch. Lead by asking:
- "How many requirements does your team process per week?"
- "How many recruiters/salespeople are working them right now?"
- "Do you currently have a single view of billing rate vs payout vs margin per job, or is that reconciled at month-end?"

Do the ROI math live, on the call, using **their** numbers — not generic numbers. A calculation built from their own inputs is much harder to dismiss than a canned pitch.

Ask which pain point hurts most *right now* — their answer tells you which module to demo first and which value point to lead with.

---

## Value points (use selectively, based on their answer above)

1. **Recruiter time recovered** — manual shortlisting typically costs 20–30 min/requirement. At 15–20 requirements/week, that's 5–6 hrs/week per recruiter recovered with AI-assisted ranked matching. Frame as recurring payroll cost saved, every week, forever — not a one-time efficiency gain.

2. **Faster submissions win more deals** — same requirement is usually being worked by multiple vendors in parallel. Speed-to-submit often decides who wins. Instant ranked shortlists vs manual spreadsheet scanning = direct win-rate impact, not just efficiency.

3. **Margin visibility that doesn't exist today** — most bench-sales shops track billing/payout/margin in scattered sheets, so leakage goes unnoticed until month-end. Live per-job margin view catches this in real time instead of retroactively.

4. **Vendor accountability** — performance stats (submissions/shortlists/placements) let them see which vendors actually convert vs just send volume. Gives them leverage in their own vendor negotiations.

5. **Scales without added headcount** — role-based dashboards mean more salespeople can be onboarded without more admin overhead, since each person sees only their scoped pipeline. Relevant since they're "starting now" — system grows with them instead of needing a rebuild at 5x volume.

6. **One-time cost vs recurring SaaS drain** — ₹4.8L is a ceiling, not a subscription, since they own the source outright. Worth contrasting explicitly against a comparable off-the-shelf ATS/staffing SaaS cost per seat/month over 2–3 years — full ownership usually wins on TCO past a handful of users.

## Pain points → what SetuHire fixes (mapped to proposal's "Problems We're Solving" table)

| Pain point they raised | What actually fixes it | Notes for the call |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter drowning in volume (200+ resumes, 90% irrelevant) | AI-assisted matching, ranked shortlist | Ask: how many hours/week currently spent just screening before real evaluation starts? Anchor on that number — it's pure waste today. |
| No structured resume data (PDFs/Word aren't queryable) | Structured, parsed candidate profiles | Often the biggest unlock in practice — difference between "search" and "hope someone remembers who had that skill." |
| JD-to-resume matching is subjective (inconsistent recruiter scoring) | Consistent ranked fit scores for everyone | Matters more as they scale — inconsistency compounds with team size and is invisible until a bad submission costs a client relationship. |
| Slow candidate communication (manual status/rejections/scheduling) | **Not in current scope.** Pipeline/status tracking is in scope; automated candidate-facing comms (auto email/SMS triggers) is not. | Be honest about this live — don't silently absorb it into the fixed price. Name it as a change request or v1.1 item if they push on it. |
| No pipeline visibility (founders chasing recruiters for status) | Role-based live dashboards | Often the point that actually closes the deal — it's the founder's own time being wasted today. |

## Fallback (if they push back on ₹4.8L)

Don't cut price on the same scope. Options, in order of preference:
1. **Trim scope, phase the rest** — defer admin reporting / finance permission-gating to a v1.1, quoted separately once v1 is live. Frame as "same core platform, smaller v1 boundary," not a discount.
2. **Extend timeline instead of cutting price** — 8 weeks instead of 6, same ₹4.8L scope.

## Internal notes (don't say out loud)

- ₹4.8L is a fixed number, not a range — a range invites negotiation down from the top; a fixed number redirects negotiation to scope instead.
- Internal cost floor: ~150–160 hrs at $30/hr (~₹3.2L–4L) — achievable because schema, matching logic, and design system are already built out.
- If they hesitate on the 25% advance despite having seen a working POC, that's a signal on how serious the commitment is.
- Don't offer "we'll move faster with AI" as a price-reduction lever — speed gained should protect margin, not lower the quote further.
- Support/AMC pricing is deliberately left open in the proposal — don't commit to a number before knowing their usage pattern.
